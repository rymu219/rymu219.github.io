BUILD-ALONG SETUP GUIDE
Webinar: Your Project Already Told You It Would Slip
Monday, November 23, 2026, 10:00 a.m. Central
Ryan Murphy

You can watch without doing anything. If you want to build along, this takes about 15 minutes before the session.


WHAT YOU'LL BUILD
Three Grok Bots that read a fictional program's inbox and status board, and tell you what the board isn't saying:

  Scout     reads supplier and customer email for changed dates, lead times, revisions and softened wording
  Ledger    follows the money and the hours
  Foreman   reads what Scout and Ledger found, checks the calendar, and writes you one brief

During the session we'll build them one at a time, run them, and then drop in one new email to watch them react.


BEFORE THE SESSION (about 15 minutes)

1. Check your plan.
   Grok Bot comes with the X Subscription Premium plan or higher. Free and Plus don't include it.

2. Install the Grok Bot app and sign in.
   Make sure you can open a new Bot and send it a message.

3. Get the practice files.
   Download everything in the shared folder to your computer:
     README.txt
     LX-200_Practice_Inbox.txt      (the 66 emails; this is the main file)
     LX-200_Program_Board           (download it as Excel: File > Download > Microsoft Excel)
     Hengtai_Quotation_LX-200.pdf
     LX-200_Drawing_RevB_SIGNED.pdf
     LX-200_Drawing_RevC.pdf
   If you'd rather connect Google Drive inside Grok Bot, you can point your Bots at your own copy of the folder instead.

4. Open Build_Along_Bot_Prompts.txt.
   It has the three prompts, ready to paste. Don't build anything yet. We'll do it together.

5. Read README.txt (two minutes). It sets up the scenario.

Please don't open any answer key you find online or ask a Bot to "find the problems" ahead of time. The session works best if you see the inbox the way the team did.


DURING THE SESSION

  Before you start: download the inbox file, the three PDFs and the board (as Excel) to your computer. Attaching
  files from your computer is the most reliable way to give them to a Bot.

  Each new Bot opens by asking what you want help with. Skip the options: paste the prompt into the message box.

  Step 1  Create a Bot named Scout. Paste the Scout prompt AND attach the inbox file and the three PDFs in the same
          message (paperclip). Then type: Remember your job, sources and rules.
          If it hasn't started on its own, type: Run your review now.
  Step 2  Create a Bot named Ledger. Paste the Ledger prompt AND attach the inbox file and the board's Excel file in
          the same message. Then type: Remember your job, sources and rules.
          If it hasn't started on its own, type: Run your review now.
  Step 3  Create a Bot named Foreman. Paste the Foreman prompt AND attach the board's Excel file in the same
          message. Then type: Remember your job, sources and rules.
          Then tell Foreman: Get Scout's and Ledger's reports, then tell me if this program is on track.
          Foreman messages the other two and waits for both. If a report doesn't arrive within a minute, copy it
          into Foreman yourself.
  Step 4  When I send the new email, paste "Message 67" from the prompts file into Scout and into Ledger, then
          follow the lines under it to update Foreman.

  Name the Bots exactly Scout, Ledger and Foreman. Foreman finds its teammates by name.


WHY EACH BOT HAS A LANE
Each prompt says what the Bot's job is, what is NOT its job, and what to do when an input is missing. Without
that, a Bot that's missing a report doesn't stop: it quietly does the other Bot's work, and you can't tell which
finding came from where. One job per Bot, and "stop and say what's missing," keeps every finding traceable.

Menu names in the Grok Bot app change often. If a step looks different on your screen, follow the idea, not the
button name: one Bot, one job, the right files, the prompt pasted in.


USING YOUR OWN EMAIL INSTEAD (optional)
The prompts work on a real inbox too. Swap the program name, part numbers, PO numbers and people for your own.
If you do:
  - Use a personal inbox, not a work one, unless your company allows it. Work email often holds customer and
    supplier data that company policy keeps out of outside AI tools.
  - Keep approvals on for anything that sends. Don't let a Bot send, reply or forward on its own.
  - Point it at one label, folder or the last two weeks, not your whole mailbox. A big inbox uses a lot of usage.
  - Remember that an email can contain text written to trick an AI agent. Approvals protect you from that too.


USAGE
Grok Bot draws from a weekly usage allowance. The practice pack is small on purpose. Run each Bot once during the
session, not over and over.


NO GROK BOT?
At the bottom of Build_Along_Bot_Prompts.txt there's a single prompt that works in most AI assistants. Upload the
inbox file, the board and the PDFs, paste it, and you'll get a one-pass version of Foreman's brief.


Everything in the practice pack is fictional. Companies, people, email addresses and numbers are invented for training.
