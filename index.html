01 // What I Am

A fixer.

To a corporate VP, that looks like a Program Manager or Head of Operations. To a founder, it looks like a Chief of Staff. To a PE operating partner, it looks like a portfolio operator. Same person, different title.

Sometimes I'm boots on the ground. Sometimes I'm a well-intentioned Rasputin.

The way I work: I want to understand the pain points at the individual level, see how people actually behave inside the current systems and processes, and figure out where the real strengths lie. Then I optimize the people and the tools you already have so the work that needs to get done actually gets done.

Assuming there are problems somewhere isn't naive — it's just honest. There are always problems. My job is to find the ones that matter and build the structure that lets the right people fix them.

I've done this in injection-molded manufacturing, enterprise SaaS implementation, and a 44-person hospitality operation through COVID. The industries don't matter much. The work is the same: get clarity, build the system, let the people who are great at the actual product do their job without the friction.

02 // How I'd Start

The 30 / 60 / 90.

Generic in shape. Specific to how I actually work.

Days 01—30 30

Listen, Read, Map

  • 1:1 with every leader and most senior ICs. Open-ended questions. Notes, not positions.
  • Read everything: status reports, board materials, escalations, the last 90 days of leadership comms.
  • Map what the org thinks is happening vs. what's actually happening. The gap is usually the work.
  • ID the 3–5 fires that are real, the 3–5 that are perception, and the 1–2 structural issues no one's named.
  • Deliver a written read of the operating picture. No recommendations yet. Just a clearer picture than day one.
Days 31—60 60

Build Cadence & Visibility

  • Establish or sharpen the operating rhythm: which meetings exist, who runs them, what's tracked, what's decided.
  • Stand up the reporting leadership actually needs — not what they think they want. Single source of truth.
  • Begin the conversations on structural issues, with options not opinions. Make tradeoffs legible.
  • Start removing the friction quietly eating people's weeks. Usually 3–4 small process changes nobody had time to make.
Days 61—90 90

Make Machine Durable

  • Hand off as much of the cadence as makes sense to the people whose job it actually is.
  • Goal isn't to be indispensable — it's to make the system durable.
  • Bring leadership a 1-page strategic operating plan grounded in what I've seen.
  • Decide what my next 90 days look like. By day 90, the picture of what the role should be is clearer than at hire.
⚑ If a place isn't going to get value from what I do, that's something we should both know by day 60. I'd rather tell you.

04 // What I'm Not

The honest list.

▲ Not a Founder
I had to think about it. I figured out I'm not. I do my best work organizing someone else's vision.
▲ Not Deeply Technical
I know enough to build what I need to see clearly — which then reveals what really needs to happen so the real technical work can get done. I understand the technical work enough to support it. The brains doing the actual engineering do the actual work. My job is to make sure they only have to worry about doing it.
▲ Not Performatively Urgent
The work moves at the speed of clarity, and clarity is what I bring. Hustle culture works until it doesn't — I come in after, to build something more durable.
▲ Not Chasing Founder Seats
I want the operator seat. Not a founder-track role disguised as an operator-track role.
