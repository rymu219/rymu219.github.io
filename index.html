02 // How I'd Start

The 30 / 60 / 90.

Generic in shape, specific to how I work. At a 2,000-person company this is 90 days. At your startup, compress it to weeks. The sequence holds, the clock just speeds up: see clearly, build the system, make it durable.

Phase 1 30

Listen, Read, Map

  • 1:1 with every leader and most senior ICs. Real questions. Notes, not positions.
  • Read whatever exists. Status, escalations, the threads where the real decisions happen.
  • Map what the org thinks is happening against what's actually happening. The gap is usually the work.
  • Name the fires that are real, the ones that are only perception, and the structural issues no one has said out loud.
  • Deliver a written read of the operating picture. No fixes yet. Just a clearer picture than day one.
Phase 2 60

Build Cadence & Visibility

  • Establish the operating rhythm. Which meetings exist, who runs them, what's tracked, what's decided.
  • Stand up the reporting leadership actually needs, not what they think they want. A single source of truth.
  • Open the structural conversations with options, not opinions. Make the tradeoffs legible.
  • Start killing the friction quietly eating people's weeks. Usually a handful of changes nobody had time to make.
Phase 3 90

Make The Machine Durable

  • Hand off the cadence to the people whose job it actually is.
  • The goal isn't to be indispensable. It's to make the system run without me.
  • Bring leadership a one-page operating plan grounded in what I've seen.
  • Decide what the next stretch looks like. By now the shape of the role is clearer than at hire.

04 // Deep Dive

I built a way to see a project failing before the project knows.

Every status report is a lagging indicator. Schedule variance, budget, percent complete. They only tell you what has gone wrong once it is already visible enough to admit. By then the system underneath has already paid for it.

So I built the Systemic Vitality Index. It measures the health of the execution system, not the performance of the project. It reads the system underneath the work and tells me whether that system can still produce reliable outcomes, before the schedule moves. It is grounded in systems thinking and quality management, in the lineage of Deming, Juran, and Senge. It was not invented from a blank page.

It scores four leading signals of execution health. Each one is a distinct way a system quietly fails.

Decision Speed
Can the team turn ambiguity into a committed decision fast enough to keep work flowing? Slow alignment is hidden drag.
Repeat Problems
Is the same failure recurring? If it is, the system is producing the problem, not encountering it. That is structural, not noise.
Info Freshness
Are people coordinating off current reality, or off an expired version of it? Stale inputs quietly poison good decisions.
Early Warning
Does bad news travel early enough to act on, or does it arrive as a surprise? A green dashboard built on silence is not health.

A dashboard tells you what happened. The SVI tells you what is about to. The difference is in the design.

  • It diagnoses and prescribes. Every score comes with a read in plain English and a specific next action, not just a number.
  • It separates confidence from score. A quiet team that logs nothing does not get to look healthy. Low data means low confidence, not high health.
  • It punishes chronic over acute. A problem that recurs for four weeks hurts far more than one bad week, because recurrence is structural and a bad week is noise.
  • It resists gaming. You cannot make it green by hiding reality, logging trivia early, or renaming the same issue. The resistance is built in from the start, because a health score people can fake is worse than no score at all.
Running Now

What it does today

  • Runs weekly across a live program portfolio.
  • Collapses a wall of status into one number, a band from Strong to Critical, a written diagnosis, and a recommended action.
  • Trends over time, so I read the direction, not a snapshot.
  • Has flagged programs as fragile while their formal status still read green. That early read is the entire point.
Where It Goes

The ideal state

  • A standalone engine any operator can point at any portfolio.
  • A pattern library, so the system recognizes what fragility looked like the last ten times, before it became failure.
  • Predictive. Name the project that will slip, and the week it will start, before anyone on it feels it.
⚑ The scoring model underneath, the weighting, the chronicity curves, the resistance to gaming, stays offline. That is the moat. What is here is what it does and why it works. If you want to watch it run, that is a conversation.
